# 02. Enterprise Information Architecture

## Overview
The Enterprise Information Architecture (EIA) defines how knowledge is created, organized, stored, shared, and governed within PROJECT PRAGYA.

## Information Domains
The platform will cover the following domains:
- Policy and regulatory knowledge
- Claims and underwriting knowledge
- Operational SOPs and process manuals
- Product and customer information
- Internal communications and approvals
- Audit, compliance, and risk records

## Architecture Layers
### 1. Source Layer
Original content sources such as PDFs, scanned documents, spreadsheets, presentations, emails, and structured systems.

### 2. Ingestion Layer
Responsible for document upload, OCR, metadata extraction, version control, and indexing.

### 3. Knowledge Layer
Stores processed knowledge in a structured and searchable form, including metadata, embeddings, tags, and relationships.

### 4. Access Layer
Provides search, browsing, role-based access, and retrieval experiences for users.

### 5. Intelligence Layer
Adds AI capabilities such as summarization, semantic search, recommendation, and decision support.

### 6. Governance Layer
Ensures quality, auditability, approval, retention, and secure access.

## Core Information Principles
- One authoritative source where possible
- Metadata must be consistent and searchable
- Content should be traceable to its source
- Sensitive content must be protected by access control
- AI outputs must remain grounded in approved data

## Expected Outcome
The architecture will support a unified enterprise knowledge platform that connects documents, context, and decision workflows in a trusted and scalable manner.
